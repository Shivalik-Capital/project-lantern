Dementia is not a single specific disease. It is a general term describing a wide range of symptoms associated with a decline in memory or other thinking skills severe enough to reduce a person's ability to perform everyday activities. Though dementia is common in old age, it can rarely affect young individuals too.

Dementia often starts with memory problems, but a person with dementia can also find it hard:
- To plan and carry out day-to-day tasks
- To communicate with others
- They may also have changes in their mood
- Ability to make decisions, or may see changes in their personality.

As dementia is 'progressive', someone with dementia will become more dependent upon others to help them as time goes on.

## Dementia and Its Types

Dementia can affect people differently depending on its type. Some common types are:

**1. Alzheimer's Disease**
This is the most common type of dementia. Difficulty remembering recent conversations, events or information is often one of the earliest signs.

**2. Vascular Dementia**
This is caused by reduced blood flow to the brain and may occur after a stroke or several small strokes. The person may have difficulty thinking, planning, concentrating or remembering, along with mood disturbances. Symptoms may begin suddenly or worsen gradually in steps.

**3. Dementia with Lewy Bodies**
People may experience changes in their thinking and alertness, which can fluctuates within a day or hours. They may also see strange images that are not actually there, develop tremors or stiffness, and have difficulty walking or frequent falls.

**4. Frontotemporal Dementia**
This type often begins at a younger age than other forms of dementia. Early signs usually include changes in behaviour, personality, emotions or language. Memory may be less affected during the early stages.

## Normal Aging vs Dementia

Many people experience some degree of forgetfulness as they grow older. Occasionally misplacing your keys, forgetting a name during a conversation, or taking a little longer to remember something are all common parts of normal ageing. This information usually comes back later, and these minor memory lapses do not interfere with everyday life or independence.

Dementia is different. It is not a normal part of ageing. Dementia is caused by diseases that damage the brain, leading to a gradual decline in memory, thinking, language, judgement, and other cognitive abilities. These changes become severe enough to interfere with a person's ability to manage everyday activities, such as taking medications, handling finances, preparing meals, or finding their way in familiar places.

While occasional forgetfulness is common with ageing, persistent and worsening difficulties that affect daily functioning should not be ignored.

### Mild Cognitive Impairment:

Mild cognitive impairment (MCI) is a condition in which a person has noticeable difficulties with memory or thinking that are greater than expected for their age. It does not interfere with an individual's everyday life in a major way and is not severe enough to be called Dementia.

Some people with MCI may develop dementia in the future, while others may remain stable or improve. Therefore, changes in memory or thinking should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

## The Situation in India

The prevalence of dementia in India is growing rapidly. According to the Longitudinal Aging Study in India (LASI), an estimated 8.8 million (88 lakhs) Indians over the age of 60 live with the condition and many remain undiagnosed.

Families often dismiss early signs of dementia as a normal part of getting old. Cultural misconceptions and limited awareness severely delays clinical diagnosis and by the time many Indian patients see a neurologist or geriatrician the disease has already progressed to moderate or severe stages.

Seeking early medical intervention provides the best chance of managing symptoms and accessing appropriate support networks like the Alzheimer's and Related Disorders Society of India (ARDSI).

## How the Brain Changes

Dementia is caused by degenerative processes that damage brain cells. This damage interferes with the ability of brain cells to communicate with each other. 

Different types of dementia are associated with particular types of brain pathology in particular regions of the brain, resulting in varied clinical presentation. The brain region called the hippocampus is the center of learning and memory and the brain cells in this region are often the first to be damaged.

## What Dementia Is Not

Given how little open conversation there is about dementia in India, it is worth clarifying some common misunderstandings:

- It is not "just old age" — while age is a risk factor, Alzheimer's or other forms is a disease, not an inevitable consequence of growing older
- It is not contagious — you cannot catch dementia from another person
- It is not caused by weakness of character or a spiritual failing — stigma around dementia in India causes real harm to patients and families; the disease is physiological
- Alzheimer's is not the only dementia — vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia, and frontotemporal dementia are distinct conditions with different presentations
- A diagnosis is not a reason to immediately withdraw from life — in the early stages, people with dementia can and do continue to participate in family life, maintain relationships, and find meaning

## Seek Early Help

Recognising the early signs of dementia and seeking medical advice at the right time can make a meaningful difference. Early assessment helps identify the possible causes of memory and thinking difficulties, allows timely access to appropriate care and support, and enables individuals and families to plan for the future.

Although dementia is a progressive condition, many interventions can help manage symptoms, maintain independence for as long as possible, and improve quality of life for both the person living with dementia and their caregivers.

Families do not have to face dementia alone. Support, information, and guidance are available through organisations such as the Alzheimer's and Related Disorders Society of India (ARDSI), Dementia Care in SCARF (DEMCARES), and the Dementia India Alliance (DIA), which work towards improving awareness, access to care, and support for people affected by dementia and their families.
