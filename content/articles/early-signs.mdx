Recognizing the early signs of Alzheimer's disease is critical for receiving a timely diagnosis and planning for the future. Many families in India dismiss early symptoms as a normal part of ageing. This delay often results in patients missing out on treatments that can manage symptoms and improve their quality of life during the early stages of the disease.

The Alzheimer's Association has developed a list of common warning signs to help families differentiate between normal age-related memory changes and potential signs of Alzheimer's disease. If you notice any of these signs in yourself or someone you love, it is important to schedule an appointment with a doctor.

## 1. Memory Loss That Disrupts Daily Life

One of the most common early signs of Alzheimer's disease is memory loss, especially forgetting recently learned information. Others include forgetting important dates or events, asking for the same information repeatedly and increasingly relying on memory aids like note pads or family members for things they used to handle on their own.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Sometimes forgetting names or appointments but remembering them later.

## 2. Challenges in Planning or Solving Problems

Some people may experience changes in their ability to develop and follow a plan or work with numbers. They may have trouble following a familiar recipe or keeping track of monthly bills. They may have difficulty concentrating and take much longer to do things than they did before.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Making occasional errors when balancing a checkbook or managing finances.

## 3. Difficulty Completing Familiar Tasks

People with Alzheimer's often find it hard to complete daily tasks. Sometimes they may have trouble driving to a familiar location, managing a budget at work or remembering the rules of a favorite game.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Occasionally needing help to use the settings on a microwave or to record a television show.

## 4. Confusion With Time or Place

People with Alzheimer's can lose track of dates, seasons and the passage of time. They may have trouble understanding something if it is not happening immediately. Sometimes they may forget where they are or how they got there.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Getting confused about the day of the week but figuring it out later.

## 5. Trouble Understanding Visual Images and Spatial Relationships

For some people, having vision problems is a sign of Alzheimer's. They may have difficulty reading, judging distance and determining color or contrast, which may cause issues with driving.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Vision changes related to cataracts.

## 6. New Problems With Words in Speaking or Writing

People with Alzheimer's may have trouble following or joining a conversation. They may stop in the middle of a conversation and have no idea how to continue or they may repeat themselves. They may struggle with vocabulary, have trouble naming a familiar object or use the wrong name.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Sometimes having trouble finding the right word.

## 7. Misplacing Things and Losing the Ability to Retrace Steps

A person with Alzheimer's disease may put things in unusual places. They may lose things and be unable to go back over their steps to find them again. Sometimes they may accuse others of stealing. This may occur more frequently over time.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Misplacing things from time to time and retracing steps to find them.

## 8. Decreased or Poor Judgment

People with Alzheimer's may experience changes in judgment or decision-making. For example, they may use poor judgment when dealing with money or pay less attention to grooming or keeping themselves clean.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Making a bad decision once in a while.

## 9. Withdrawal From Work or Social Activities

A person with Alzheimer's may experience changes in the ability to hold or follow a conversation. As a result, they may withdraw from hobbies, social activities or other engagements. They may have trouble keeping up with a favorite team or activity.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Sometimes feeling weary of work, family and social obligations.

## 10. Changes in Mood and Personality

Individuals with Alzheimer's may experience mood and personality changes. They can become confused, suspicious, depressed, fearful or anxious. They may be easily upset at home, with friends or when out of their comfort zone.

**What is typical age-related change?**
Developing very specific ways of doing things and becoming irritable when a routine is disrupted.

## Next Steps

If you notice one or more of these signs in yourself or another person, it can be difficult to know what to do. It is natural to feel uncertain or nervous about discussing these changes with others. 

The first and most important step is to consult a healthcare professional. A doctor can evaluate your symptoms, determine their cause and provide guidance on the next steps. Early detection allows you to explore treatments that may provide some relief of symptoms and help you maintain a level of independence longer.
